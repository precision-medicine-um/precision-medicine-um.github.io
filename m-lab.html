M-Lab · Translational research

From Targeted Therapy to Living Biologicals

The M-Lab is a translational research laboratory developing tumour-targeted therapies and advanced disease models to improve diagnosis and treatment while protecting healthy tissue. Core work includes hypoxia-activated prodrugs, tumour-specific immunotherapy, ScanNanoTreat, therapeutic bacteria, and multimorbidity models.

01 — Research line

Targeted Therapy

Developing treatments that specifically target tumours while protecting surrounding healthy tissue.

  • Hypoxia-Activated Prodrugs (HAPs): Studying cancer-specific treatments like CP-506 (currently in clinical trials) that convert into toxic derivatives exclusively within the hypoxic areas of tumours.
  • Tumour-Specific Immunotherapy: Investigating targeted agents such as the L19-IL2 cytokine, which is currently undergoing clinical trials.
  • Metabolic Alterations in Tumours: Elucidating the mechanistic role of local and systemic metabolic dysregulation in tumour progression and therapy resistance, and translating these findings into new therapeutic strategies, currently focusing on increased extracellular cathepsin activity and excessive intracellular cholesterol build-up.
  • ScanNanoTreat Radiotherapy: Advancing a combined approach for simultaneous imaging and treatment of solid tumours (initially breast and pancreatic cancers) to reduce radiation doses by over 30%.
  • Polyphenol Applications: Researching the dual use of polyphenols as radioprotectors and radiosensitisers.
02 — Research line

Living Biologicals

Engineering and utilising bacteria for clinical and industrial applications.

  • Bacterial Genetic Engineering: Developing innovative tools to successfully insert specific genes of interest into bacteria.
  • Clinical Anticancer Therapies: Utilising engineered bacteria for localised tumour treatment via CDEPT (Clostridium Directed Enzymatic Prodrug Therapy) and CMIT (Clostridium Mediated ImmunoTherapy).
  • Industrial Applications: Applying engineered bacteria in non-clinical settings, specifically focusing on the production of biogas.
Supporting both research lines

Innovative Models

Developing advanced models and imaging techniques to study diseases and their complex interactions.

  • Multimorbidity Animal Models: Creating novel models to research interactions between vascular diseases, cancer, and cognitive decline.
  • Chicken Chorioallantoic Membrane (CAM)-Xenograft Model: Using the vascularised extra-embryonic membrane in the fertilised chicken egg to support the growth of human cancer cells and patient-derived tumour tissue, to test novel treatment strategies.
  • Clinical and In Vitro Studies: Investigating links between cancer and vascular calcification, including plasma-based thrombosis and cellular damage analyses in breast cancer.
  • Advanced Imaging Probes: Developing probes such as 18F-HX4 and 99mTc-labelled anti-CEACAM5-VHH for CT, PET, SPECT, and BLI.
  • SMART-RT: Utilising image-guided, high-precision small animal irradiators to test targeted drug and radiation therapies with minimal off-target impact.
